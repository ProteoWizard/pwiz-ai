mean(best-N) on a second dataset: the gain does not replicate

TDP-43 Plasma EV-Quant, 163 Astral DIA files, entrapment oracle, pass-1 experiment-wide scope · arms measured so far: max, N=2, N=3, N=4, N=6

On the 82-file SEA-AD cohort mean(best-N) recovered +14.6% at N=2 and peaked at +16.4% at N=6. On this dataset, at twice the file count, N=2 is −0.20% and the curve only falls from there. The optimum is N = 1 — plain max. There is no depth that pays.

The lever is not failing to engage — it engages exactly as designed. mean-best-2 demotes 76% of the single-run acceptances (1,407 → 340) and drives that slice's measured FDP from 4.24% down to 1.78%, essentially to nominal. SEA-AD 82f saw a comparable engagement (−69.6%).

What differs is how much error that buys back. 1,407 singletons at 4.24% false is about 60 recoverable false identifications. At SEA-AD 82f the singleton slice was 20.6% false — roughly 5× the FDR headroom per singleton removed. So here the transform fires correctly, frees almost nothing, and pays 61 net identifications for it.

Correction, 2026-08-01. An earlier draft put the single-run slice at 1.40% and reported that mean-best-2 demoted only ~10% of it. 5 · Does the left-side pile-up need biology, or false discovery?

The six Pool injections are the same sample repeated, so between-sample biological variability is zero by construction. The control is the six donors at acquisition positions 70–75, immediately before the first pools. Both are Stage-5-only arms off the same linked parquets.

Technical replicates reproduce far better on the right (48.0% vs 33.1% detected in every run), which confirms the contrast works. On the left, three conclusions: (a) not mostly biology — the pools still carry 10.3% singletons with zero biological variability, so genuine between-donor biology explains only the ~3.8‑point difference; (b) not mostly false discovery — the k=1 slice measures 1.34% / 1.17% FDP, essentially the 1% nominal, so it is being controlled correctly; (c) what remains is detection-limit stochasticity, real low-abundance peptides crossing threshold in some injections and not others.

Caveats. Six runs is a weak instrument — k takes only six values. And the six pools are not pure technical replicates: three sit at positions 76–78 and three at 161–163, so they carry instrument drift across most of the series. Pure injection-to-injection stochasticity would therefore be lower than 10.3%, and correspondingly more of the donor singletons would be biological than the 3.8‑point figure implies. The direction holds; the split is softer than the numbers look.

6 · What this measurement does and does not control

questionstatus
Could the pass-2 mode differ between the datasets and move these pass-1 numbers? No — excluded by construction. The pass-1 model-diagnostics report is written after first-pass Percolator and before first-pass protein FDR (FirstJoinTask.cs:1877), while every OSPREY_PASS2_QVALUE-conditioned structure — including the protein-compact stratum — is built from the protein-FDR result that runs afterwards. Pass-2 mode cannot reach this view.
Could the build or the Stage-5-only shortcut have moved the baseline? No — measured. The max arm reproduces the completed 18-hour pipeline's own max arm bit-for-bit on all 12 extracted fields, across two different binaries and two different execution paths.
Are all arms otherwise identical? Yes. Same 163 files, same Stage 1–4 parquets (hard-linked from one pool), same library, same pick model, same pass-2 mode. OSPREY_EXPERIMENT_AGG is the only variable.
Is “plasma EV matrix” separable from “library built for a different tissue”? NO — these are confounded here. This cohort is plasma EV searched against the SEA-AD brain-derived carafe library. A poorly-matched library would also plausibly yield a small, clean single-run population. This dataset therefore shows that the removable population can be absent, and that the A×B screen detects it in advance — but it cannot attribute the cause to the matrix specifically. Separating them needs a third dataset with a matched library.

7 · The consequence for a default

Dataset 1's own strongest caveat was that cohort composition matters more than cohort size. Dataset 2 makes that concrete and costly: TDP-43 at 163 files has a max-arm union efficiency of 67.2% — already as high as SEA-AD's best-2 efficiency at 82 files, with twice the files. The scale-driven efficiency collapse that this lever exists to repair is not a function of file count at all.

So “many runs” is not the precondition for turning it on. The precondition is a leaky single-run population, and that is a property of the matrix, the library and the instrument series — not of N. On plasma EV searched against a brain-derived library, that population is absent and the lever is a straight sensitivity tax: −2.09% at the 1% reported-q operating point a user actually ships, for a calibration improvement of 0.04 points.

Recommendation on the evidence so far: mean(best-N) should not be made the default. It should stay opt-in, gated per dataset. The most promising gate is no longer the A×B screen (withdrawn above) but the reproducibility frontier already rendered in every model-diagnostics report: if its experiment-wide yield curve peaks at k=1, requiring reproducibility — whether as a hard filter or as a mean(best-N) score transform — has nothing to offer. On this dataset it peaks at k=1 and the arms agree. That correspondence is one dataset deep and needs testing wherever mean(best-N) did pay.

Two datasets is not enough to settle a default either way; this one is a single draw, exactly as the 82-file +14.6% was. What it does establish is that the sign can flip — so any future recommendation has to come with a per-dataset test rather than a blanket “on above N files” rule, and the cheapest such test we have is already validated.

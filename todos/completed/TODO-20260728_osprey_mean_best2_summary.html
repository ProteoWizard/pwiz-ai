mean(best-N): one dataset where it pays, one where it does not

Two-dataset synthesis · SEA-AD Pilot-MTG (82 files) vs TDP-43 Plasma EV-Quant (163 files) · first-pass experiment-wide scope, entrapment oracle

mean(best-N) replaces the experiment-wide “best score over runs” with the mean of a precursor's best N per-run scores, so that a detection resting on a single run is demoted. On SEA-AD it recovered +14.6% more identifications at N=2 and peaked at +16.4%. On TDP-43, at twice the file count, it is −0.2% at N=2 and only falls from there.

The two results are not in conflict, and the difference is not cohort size, cohort quality or statistical noise. A reproducibility lever can only pay when irreproducibility is a dominant source of the surviving false discoveries. That condition held on SEA-AD and does not hold on TDP-43 — and it can be checked from a single ordinary run, before committing to any A/B.

2 · Why they differ

A reproducibility lever needs two things to be true at once. Both held on dataset 1; neither holds on dataset 2.

conditionSEA-AD 82fTDP-43 163f
(a) The single-run population is substantially false — there is error worth removing k=1 slice 20.6% false† k=1 slice 4.24% false — about 60 recoverable false identifications in total
(b) Reproducibility discriminates — false things fail to repeat assumed and consistent with the gain 76.9% of measured false hits repeat in ≥2 runs, 55.4% in ≥6 — but this figure is contaminated, see §3.1. Treat as unreliable.

† Dataset 1's k-slice figures are quoted as published. Their array-indexing convention has not been re-verified against the source run (see “Open” below); dataset 2's have been, against the rendered report.

The lever is not failing to engage on TDP-43. It demotes 76% of the single-run acceptances (1,407→340) and pulls that slice's measured FDP from 4.24% to 1.78%, essentially to the 1% nominal — comparable engagement to SEA-AD's −69.6%. It fires correctly, frees roughly 60 false identifications, and costs 61 real ones. The mechanism works; the payoff is not there.

3.1 · Why the entrapment column is contaminated

Entrapment in this library is generated by shuffling each target sequence; decoys are generated by reversing it. Shuffling preserves positional identity far more readily than reversal, and for low-complexity sequences it is close to a no-op. Measured over a 1,000,000-pair sample of the pairing manifest:

vs its own targetmedian identityp99max >0.50 (OpenSWATH reject)>0.25
entrapment (shuffled)0.1940.6250.929 2.55%29.6%
decoy (reversed)0.1430.5000.9090.92% 16.9%

Roughly 40,000 entrapment sequences across the library exceed the 0.50 threshold. The tail is not subtle — GGWGGGGGGWGGGGGGGGGWGGGGGGGR → GGWGGGGGGWGWGGGGGGGGGGGGGGGR (0.929), EEEEEEEEEEEREK → EEEEEEEEEEREEK (0.857). Such a peptide has, by construction, the same precursor mass as its target, a near-identical b/y ladder (reordering a run of identical residues does not move fragment masses), and the same retention behaviour. It is detected wherever its target is detected — in every run.

Consequence. A near-copy entrapment hit reproduces because it is shadowing a real peptide, not because false discoveries reproduce. So the 55.4% overstates how much of the genuinely-false population repeats, by an amount not yet measured. Condition (b) above should be treated as unproven. Condition (a) is unaffected and if anything strengthened: the same contamination inflates entrapment counts generally, so entrapment-derived FDP is biased HIGH and the true single-run slice is cleaner than 4.24% — leaving even less for the lever to recover. The measured curve is unaffected either way, since both arms are scored against the same oracle and the bias cancels in the comparison.

This applies to dataset 1 as well, which used the same library family, so neither dataset's k-slice FDP figures are clean. It does not plausibly reverse a +14.6% gain, but it does mean the mechanism story for that gain rests on a contaminated statistic too.

4 · What the left-hand pile-up actually is

The per-run view of this dataset looks alarming: 10,450 single-run detections (23% of the union) measuring 38.2% FDP, and a union that never levels off. That is the picture that motivates a reproducibility lever. But it describes the population before experiment-wide control, and the ordinary max gate already removes most of it — down to 1,407 singletons at 4.24%.

To ask whether what remains is real, two 6-file cohorts were run: the six Pool injections (one sample, six times — zero biological variability by construction) against six donors from adjacent acquisition positions.

  • Not mostly biology. The pools retain 10.3% singletons with zero biological variability; donor-to-donor biology explains only the ~3.8‑point difference.
  • Not mostly false discovery. The k=1 slice measures 1.34% / 1.17% FDP — essentially the 1% nominal. It is being controlled correctly.
  • What remains is measurement variability and the detection limit — real, low-abundance peptides crossing threshold in some injections and not others.

Caveats. Six runs is a weak instrument — k takes only six values. And the six pools are not pure technical replicates: three sit at acquisition positions 76–78 and three at 161–163, so they carry instrument drift across most of the series. Pure injection-to-injection stochasticity is therefore below 10.3%, and correspondingly more of the donor singletons are biological than the 3.8‑point figure implies. The direction holds; the split is softer than the numbers look.

The frontier reading is a yes/no; the entrapment spread is graded, so it also says how much signal is present. Note both are one dataset deep as gates — they should be tested on a cohort where mean(best-N) did pay before being relied on.

6 · The lesson

For a lever to add value in FDR control it must act on a dominant source of the surviving false discoveries. On TDP-43 irreproducibility is not that source. Three things account for it: ordinary measurement variability; a high proportion of genuine peptides sitting near the instrument detection limit, which makes real identifications sporadic; and a substantial population of false identifications that reproduce anyway.

The corollary matters more than the individual numbers. File count does not predict whether the lever helps. TDP-43 has twice SEA-AD's files and a max-arm union efficiency of 67.2% — already as high as SEA-AD's best-2 efficiency at 82 files. The scale-driven efficiency loss this lever repairs is a property of the matrix, the library and the instrument series, not of N. So a rule of the form “enable above N files” is not supportable on this evidence; a per-dataset check is.

Recommendation: keep OSPREY_EXPERIMENT_AGG opt-in. Two datasets are not enough to settle a default in either direction — but they are enough to show the sign can flip, which is what rules out a blanket default.

7 · Open, and what would settle it

  • Re-read dataset 1's crossRun arrays under a checked convention. These arrays have length n and are indexed by k−1; an earlier analysis of dataset 2 read them as if indexed by k, reporting every k=2 quantity under the k=1 label. Dataset 2 has been corrected and verified against its rendered report; dataset 1's published k-slice figures have not been re-checked. This also decides whether the A×B screen from dataset 1 survives — on corrected dataset-2 indexing it predicts +4.9% against −0.2% observed, so it is currently withdrawn as unvalidated.
  • Test both gates where the lever paid. The frontier and entrapment-spread checks agree with the arms on the dataset where mean(best-N) fails; neither has been tried on one where it succeeds.
  • Apply a sequence-identity filter to the library-decoy path. Osprey already implements the right test — OSPREY_DECOY_MAX_SEQ_IDENTITY (OpenSWATH's shuffle_sequence_identity_threshold) and OSPREY_DECOY_MAX_FRAG_OVERLAP (EncyclopeDIA's getSmartDecoy gate). Both are default-OFF and were judged not worth enabling after the 2026-07-25 gendecoy experiment, where they barely bound: 52–522 exclusions in 494,495. But that was measured on REVERSED decoys. On the shuffled entrapment of this library the same 0.50 gate binds ~28x harder (2.55% vs 0.92%). The gate is not wrong; it was evaluated on the one generation method that has little for it to do. It is currently applied to neither the entrapment nor the decoys of an externally-built libdecoy library, and it needs to apply to both.
  • Entrapment from another species, for both entrapment and decoys. Removes the same-precursor-mass co-isolation route entirely and gives decoys realistic fragment behaviour rather than reversed ladders. Currently on hold. It would not address the remaining gap: a real absent human peptide whose isoforms and modified forms are present has no analogue in either design, and auditing that needs entrapment attributed to present proteins.
  • Re-measure the entrapment run-count distribution after filtering. Splitting the accepted-entrapment k-histogram by target identity would say directly how much of the 55.4% is near-copy shadowing. It needs per-precursor run counts, which the model-diagnostics report aggregates away — so it means either joining the per-file .1st-pass.fdr_scores.bin sidecars by entry_id, or rebuilding the library with the identity gate applied and re-running. The second is a full re-run; the first is a day of tooling.
  • Cohort composition on dataset 2. Nine Total-* samples (acquisition positions 152–160) yield 4,371 identifications against a 10,095 median — a different preparation, not instrument decay (the pools that follow them are normal). A 154-file EV-only cohort would test whether that heterogeneity suppresses the gain. Not yet run.

Osprey demultiplexing through ProteoWizard

The alternative to pwiz PR #4710: one demultiplexer, in ProteoWizard, shared by msconvert, Skyline and Osprey. Updated 2026-10-10 with replicated searches and speed measured on the final code. Sources: TODO-20261008_osprey_pwiz_demux.md, pr4710-comparison.md, pr1b-root-cause.md in pwiz-ai.

The proposal. #4710 adds staggered-window and MSX demultiplexing to Osprey with its own engine (Osprey.Demux, Osprey.DemuxTool; about 9,600 added lines of product code). ProteoWizard already has a demultiplexer, the one msconvert users rely on. Making it correct, fast and reachable through MsDataFileImpl lets Osprey's --demux auto be 559 added lines of Osprey product code (plus 546 in pwiz, shared by every tool). Measured on six Eclipse runs it identifies as many precursors as either #4710 engine, and it reads a run as fast as #4710's fastest engine.

How the gap closed. A dotTrace profile showed the solve parallelized well (about 380 s of CPU across 32 threads) while the wall time was set by the serial read. In it, the Thermo reader's precursor lookup took 22 s by itself: every DIA scan carries "Master Scan Number: 0", which matches no scan, and the lookup walked back to the first scan of the run for every spectrum to find that out. #4817 returns at once. It speeds up every Thermo DIA read, not only demultiplexing: plain msconvert of EV13 goes from 29.4 s to 21.9 s with identical mzML. What remains is the solve, nearly half of it MathNet natural-spline construction; a closed form would cut it further but changes low-order bits, so it would be a separate, gated change.

What the comparisons found

1. NNLS iteration limit (C# port bug) - #4805

On the Eclipse slice, 17 NNLS columns hit the 50-iteration cap; the C# port zeroed them, dropping 26 real peaks (up to 5.0e7) from 23 spectra. Keeping the last feasible solution, as C++ does, makes all 408 spectra match C++ at float32 precision.

2. Search-span regression from 2017 - #4814

C++'s SpectrumPeakExtractor is Skyline's TransitionBinner loop, line for line, but subtracts the largest bin half-width where Skyline subtracts the full width. Every bin above half the spectrum's highest m/z therefore accepted only part of its window: at the top, -10 to 0 ppm instead of +/-10. Against ground truth (fragments of identified peptides), the fix sends 92% of high-m/z fragment intensity to the correct window, against 69% before.

3. Why the search-span fix first looked like a loss

At Osprey's default 300,000-row training cap the corrected span lost 9.5% +/- 3.3% of precursors (five seeds), although its spectra and features are as good or better. The loss was the first-pass Percolator training, where about 1,000 positives drive the classifier and near-tied choices decide it. At 1.5M rows the corrected span is +1.0% and the seed spread falls from 6-11% to 1-3%. Filed as #4812; it is why every comparison above uses three seeds at 1.5M.

4. Thermo master-scan lookup - #4817

The speed section above: an O(n²) walk in the Thermo reader, 22 s of a 40 s read, found by profiling.

Osprey demultiplexing through ProteoWizard

The alternative to pwiz PR #4710: one demultiplexer, in ProteoWizard, shared by msconvert, Skyline and Osprey. Working draft, 2026-10-10. Sources: TODO-20261008_osprey_pwiz_demux.md, pr4710-comparison.md, pr1b-root-cause.md.

The proposal. #4710 adds staggered-window and MSX demultiplexing to Osprey with a new engine of about 10,000 lines (Osprey.Demux, Osprey.DemuxTool). ProteoWizard already has a demultiplexer, the one msconvert users rely on. Making it fast, correct and reachable through MsDataFileImpl lets Osprey's --demux auto be about 560 lines of Osprey code, and every fix lands in one place for every tool. The comparison with #4710 that motivated this also found and fixed real defects in ProteoWizard's demultiplexer, including one that has been in msconvert since 2017.

What the comparisons found

1. NNLS iteration limit (C# port bug) - #4805

On the Eclipse slice, 17 NNLS columns hit the 50-iteration cap; the C# port zeroed them, dropping 26 real peaks (up to 5.0e7) from 23 spectra. Keeping the last feasible solution, as C++ does, makes all 408 spectra match C++ at float32 precision. Six-run search: +5.4% precursors (single seed; see the caveat below).

2. Search-span regression from 2017 - #4814

C++'s SpectrumPeakExtractor is Skyline's TransitionBinner loop, line for line, but subtracts the largest bin half-width where Skyline subtracts the full width. Every bin above half the spectrum's highest m/z therefore accepted only part of its window: at the top, -10 to 0 ppm instead of +/-10. Neighbour peaks centre on 0 ppm (median within +/-0.07 ppm), so this discards about half the real matching intensity there. Against ground truth (fragments of identified peptides), the fix sends 92% of high-m/z fragment intensity to the correct window, against 69% before.

3. Why the fix first looked like a loss

The corrected span lost 9.5% +/- 3.3% of precursors over five seeds on six Eclipse runs, although its spectra and its Osprey features are as good or better (scored under any common frozen model, within +/-1% on every run). The loss was Osprey's first-pass Percolator training at its default 300,000-peak cap, where only about 1,000 positives drive the classifier and near-tied choices (initial feature, regularization C) decide it. With five times the training data the corrected span is +1.0% (three seeds), and the seed-to-seed spread falls from 6-11% to 1-3%. Filed as #4812.

What this means for comparing with #4710

Every head-to-head number so far (pwiz + NNLS fix 38,088 against #4710 weighted 36,453 and msconvert-style 35,664; covered windows -7.8%) is a single seed at the 300k cap, where the seed alone moves an arm 6-11%. They are not yet evidence either way. The comparison to present is replicated (three seeds or more) at a training cap where the variance is down, ideally with Mike's EV library.

4The anomaly worth a decision

On SEA-AD (82 files), the sampler alone gave +24.7% at matched FDP and our library alone gave +10.5%. Here the two together give +4.58% at pass 1 and +8.94% at pass 2. Two independently positive changes composing to less than either is the finding. Candidates: the gains do not compose on this matrix; plasma EV sits nearer a ceiling than brain tissue; or one of the two is negative on this dataset while positive on SEA-AD. Only the deferred A/B — the baseline’s delivered library on pickrun3, another ~19 h — separates them.

A prediction made mid-run and falsified: because the training max-bias grows with batch size (mean coelution_sum 1.19 at one file → 5.75 at 82) and TDP-43 is double SEA-AD’s batch, the gain here was predicted at ≥+24.7%. It came in at +4.58%, about one fifth. The mechanism is real; the extrapolation was not.

Separation more than halves — and that is the change working, not a regression. The old sampler’s training rows are cross-run maxima: inflated, easy, and unlike the per-run rows the model then scores. pickrun3’s rows match the scored population, so training-set separation must fall. SVM C = 1 on all three folds in both arms, so model complexity is identical and only the training distribution changed.

The 3-fold cross-validation on N training entries line is not a pickrun3 signal: both arms hit the 300,000-row cap (OspreyEnvironment.cs:350), 299,998 vs 299,997. pickrun3 changes which run represents each precursor, not how many rows are drawn.

Wall-clock deltas are an upper bound, and the two arms are not configuration-identical. The baseline ran with OSPREY_LOG_MEMORY=1 (999 [MEM] probe lines, each forcing a GC.Collect() pair) and this run ran without it (0 lines) — an unrecorded shell difference. The baseline therefore paid forced-GC overhead and was still faster, so our overhead is understated; and its memory peaks were measured under repeated forced collection, so our memory advantage is understated too. Fixed for future runs: the runner now owns this as -LogMemory, exported in both directions and recorded. The baseline’s SecondPassFDR figure is also not comparable — that run crashed in BlibWriter..ctor after 1,031 min and needed a separate 29-minute recovery node; this run completed in one pass.

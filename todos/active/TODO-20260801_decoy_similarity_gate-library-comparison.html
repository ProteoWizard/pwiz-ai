5. The lost fragments are the weak ones

Sampled: 14,958 matched precursors selected by sequence hash (unbiased by file position), yielding 9,221 ions present in the delivered library and absent from ours.

Read the aggregate histogram with care. Binning all rows by intensity shows a near-uniform 0.93–0.95 ratio in every band, which looks like the loss is intensity-indifferent. It is not. That view mixes two populations: roughly 2.3% of the deficit is whole precursors being absent, and those remove ions at every intensity, flattening the band ratios. Only the matched-precursor join above answers "what do we lose from a spectrum we still have".

12. A mechanism that was proposed and refuted

It was proposed that Mike's ungated entrapment is suppressed — that near-copies lose their chromatographic peak to the source target and go unreported, biasing his FDP optimistic and making our higher FDP an artifact of a fairer null. Directly measured and refuted: of 12,033 reported entrapment in the delivered arm, 8,741 (72.7%) have their paired target also reported. Both members of a co-assigned pair are normally reported — the signal is counted twice, not suppressed. There is therefore no optimistic bias from that mechanism, and our 0.693% vs 0.435% FDP gap must be taken at face value.

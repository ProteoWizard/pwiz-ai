# Demux modeling and a first real-data test (night of 2026-09-25)

Question: does per-product-ion-channel demultiplexing (the spec's y = A x, x >= 0, one channel at a
time, no grouping of fragments into precursors) improve quantitation on Orbitrap staggered and ZT Scan
data, and which form of it?

Code and results: `ai/.tmp/sessions/20260923-osprey-demux/model/` (not committed).
- `demux_model.py`: the model. `rho_contrast.py`: the shared-prior check.
- `ztscan_real.py`, `Run-Slices.ps1`, `Search-Slices.ps1`, `Compare-ZtScanSlices.py`: the real-data test.

## The model

- **Peptides:** 100 tryptic target peptides from the ZT Scan FASTA, charge 2 or 3, precursors
  600-660 m/z, apexes spread over one minute.
  - Top 20 b/y fragments each, with log-normal relative intensities.
  - EMG elution: FWHM 2.4 s for ZT Scan (DIA-NN's median on these runs) and 7 s for Eclipse (from
    Osprey's peak boundaries).
- **Background:** co-eluting, co-isolated peptides from the same FASTA, sized to the real peak density.
  - 20,000 for ZT Scan: about 3,100 fragment peaks per spectrum, against about 5,000 measured.
  - 2,500 for Eclipse: about 1,700 peaks per 12 Th window, matching the measured 860 per 6 Th bin.
  - The background does not change between samples.
- **Two samples:** targets change by log2 FC of -1, 0, 0 or +1.
- **Counting noise:** Poisson.
  - ZT Scan: 1 to 1,000 base-fragment ions per event at the apex (about 100 counts per ion here).
  - Eclipse: 10 to 10,000.
- **Acquisition:**
  - ZT Scan: the measured transmission (flat within ±2 Th, zero by ±9.5 Th), 429 encoded bins of
    1.18 Th, events 2.01 ms apart in a 0.971 s cycle.
  - Eclipse: 12 Th windows at k = 2 with 0.3 Th edge ramps, 101 windows per 5.45 s cycle.
- **Channels:** only those of the targets' top 6 fragments (10 ppm) are simulated and solved, with
  every peptide that contributes to them. Channels are solved independently, so this is exact.
- **Scoring:** within each target's peak (own elution at least 1% of its apex).
  - `|log2 A|`: quantity error against the target's own signal.
  - `FC err`: fold-change error on the targets that change.
  - `FC slope`: measured log2 FC regressed on true; 1 means no ratio compression.
  - `XIC r`: fragment XIC correlation with the truth.
  - `oracle`: the noiseless narrow-window measurement, meaning a perfect demux. It still carries
    other peptides in the same bin that share a channel.

## Findings

### 1. With no background there is nothing for demux to win
In isolation, the acquired spectra quantify almost perfectly, because the only interference is shared
fragment m/z. Every conclusion below comes from the realistic-background runs.

### 2. Eclipse staggered: per-channel demux works, and the separable model is best

| | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | XIC r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.345 | 0.223 | 0.65 | 0.98 |
| per-time (makima interpolation, then solve; the msconvert approach) | 0.120 | 0.093 | 0.79 | 0.98 |
| separable | 0.091 | 0.097 | 0.85 | 0.99 |
| oracle | 0.020 | 0.030 | 0.81 | 1.00 |

- Demux halves the fold-change error and removes most of the ratio compression.
- **Separable** means that, per channel and per block of time, each source has one bin distribution
  for the whole block times its own elution profile. It is at or near the oracle.
- The oracle has no counting noise, so its lower FC err is partly noise, not all demux error.

### 2b. Eclipse with the Orbitrap charge floor: demux still helps, and the simple solver wins
Counts below 8 ions censored to zero (spec §6.2):

| | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | unchanged scatter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.374 | 0.185 | 0.69 | 0.015 |
| per-time (interpolate, then solve) | 0.132 | 0.123 | 0.89 | 0.019 |
| separable | 0.148 | 0.143 | 1.04 | 0.026 |

- The censored zeros in a peak's tails distort the separable model's fixed bin distribution, and it
  overshoots (slope 1.04).
- Interpolate-then-solve, PR #4710's approach, is the better choice on real Orbitrap data unless the
  separable model learns to treat censored zeros as censored.

### 3. ZT Scan: one 1.18 Th bin is not recoverable; 3 bins centered on the precursor's bin are

| | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | XIC r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0.982 | 0.453 | 0.48 | 0.93 |
| per-sweep NNLS, own bin | 0.881 | 0.317 | 0.87 | 0.74 |
| **per-sweep NNLS, 3 bins** | **0.248** | **0.203** | **0.82** | **0.96** |
| separable, own bin | 0.899 | 0.612 | 1.00 | 0.93 |
| separable, 3 bins | 0.497 | 0.307 | 0.76 | 0.92 |
| oracle, own bin | 0.001 | 0.000 | 0.86 | 1.00 |
| oracle, 3 bins | 0.186 | 0.131 | 0.69 | 1.00 |

- **Why one bin fails:** the ZT Scan transmission has sloped edges, so a precursor's column of `A`
  depends on its exact m/z, not only on its bin. Orbitrap windows have sharp edges, so every m/z
  inside a bin gives the same column.
  - Noiseless, 29% of a precursor's signal lands in a neighboring bin. Solving on 4 subbins per bin
    cuts that to 10%.
  - Under counting noise, a single sweep cannot place a fragment within one 1.18 Th bin. The split
    between neighbors flips from sweep to sweep, which is the failure seen on the real data.
- **With 3 bins centered on the precursor's bin** (Mike's suggestion), per-sweep NNLS cuts the
  fold-change error from 0.45 to 0.20 and raises the slope from 0.48 to 0.82, close to what a perfect
  demux at that width allows.
- **Unlike Eclipse, the separable model does worse here.** Its rank selection (BIC, about 420
  deviance units per added source) keeps weak sources from being modeled on their own. At this
  density most channels have several sources, and the weak targets get absorbed.
- **A lighter penalty helps, but per-sweep still wins.** With AIC and up to 5 sources, the separable
  model's 3-bin output improves to |log2 A| 0.32 and FC err 0.28, but its slope is 0.63 against
  0.82. It also runs 2.5x slower.
  - So the rank penalty is only part of the gap.
  - The rest is likely how it handles many overlapping sources: the fixed rank cap, a bin
    distribution held constant over the whole block, and local optima in the alternating fit.

### 3b. Poisson row weights help ZT Scan, above all at low abundance
Per-sweep NNLS refit with row weights `1 / max(mu_i, 0.5)` from a first unweighted fit (spec §6.2):

| 3-bin output | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | XIC r | unchanged scatter | lowest third: unchanged / changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-sweep | 0.248 | 0.203 | 0.82 | 0.96 | 0.078 | 0.40 / 0.55 |
| per-sweep, weighted | 0.224 | 0.190 | 0.75 | 0.97 | 0.046 | 0.17 / 0.44 |

- Weighting cuts the added noise of demux: the scatter of unchanged targets falls from 0.078 to
  0.046, and in the least abundant third from 0.40 to 0.17.
- It costs some ratio compression: slope 0.82 to 0.75.
- The noise is what costs identifications on the real data, so it is tested there too (below).

### 4. An L2 roughness penalty in time spreads intensity across bins
- Fitting each bin's time course with a second-difference penalty (the spec's §4.3 smoothness, as
  first implemented) put 63% of intensity in unoccupied bins.
- The penalty is smallest when a peak is split across neighboring bins, so it actively spreads.
- Time structure has to enter as "the source does not move in m/z", which is the separable model,
  not as smoothness.

### 5. A shared signal map, rho(bin, time), cannot help
This was the candidate for borrowing information across fragments without grouping them.
- At realistic density, a target is a median 3% (ZT Scan) and 1% (Eclipse) of its own bin's total
  fragment current at its apex.
- rho in its bin versus the neighbors differs by a log2 median of 0.19 and 0.14, with a wide spread.
- The map reflects everything else eluting, not the channel's source. Dropped.

### 6. Discretization and output width, for the spec
- Output bins for ZT Scan should be reported as 3 encoded bins centered on each bin: 1.18 Th labels
  with 3.5 Th specificity.
- Orbitrap staggered keeps its boundary-union bins.

### 6b. The ZT Scan transmission must be measured well, per file
Per-sweep 3-bin demux with the simulated transmission 10% narrower or wider than the one `A` assumes:

| true transmission vs assumed | none: FC err | 3 bins: FC err | slope | \|log2 A\| | XIC r |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| matched | 0.45 | 0.20 | 0.82 | 0.25 | 0.96 |
| 10% narrower | 0.53 | 0.25 | 0.95 | 0.30 | 0.97 |
| 10% wider | 0.59 | 0.24 | 0.66 | 0.46 | 0.87 |

- The gain survives a 10% error, but a transmission wider than assumed costs accuracy and XIC shape:
  its tails spill into neighboring bins.
- This supports the spec's §2.4 per-file calibration from abundant fragments, and a gate on its
  accuracy.

### 7. Demux trades precision for accuracy, so replicate CVs cannot show its benefit
Fold-change error split into targets that change and targets that do not (the precision analog):

| | unchanged: median \|log2 FC\| | changed: median FC error |
|---|---|---|
| ZT Scan, none | 0.028 | 0.453 |
| ZT Scan, per-sweep 3 bins | 0.078 | 0.203 |
| Eclipse, none | 0.013 | 0.223 |
| Eclipse, per-time | 0.021 | 0.093 |
| Eclipse, separable | 0.025 | 0.095 |

- Interference is identical in every replicate of one sample. It biases ratios between samples but
  leaves replicate precision untouched.
- What replicate CVs see is only the noise demux adds: little on Eclipse, about 3x on ZT Scan.
- Validating the quantitation gain on real data needs samples that differ by known amounts: a two-
  or three-proteome mix, a dilution series, or ZT Scan and Zeno SWATH on matched samples (spec
  G6.1-G6.3). The ZT Scan data here is three replicates of condition A only.

## Real data: a ZT Scan slice searched with DIA-NN

- **Slice:** cycles 248-372 (RT about 4.0-6.0 min), precursors 500-700 m/z (169 encoded bins), in
  each of A1, D1 and G1. Scored on precursors with RT 4.1-5.9 min.
- **Arms:** all written by the same mzML writer from the msconvert vendor-centroided mzML, with the
  zero-intensity flanks dropped.
  - raw: the acquired spectra, searched as plain DIA and with `--scanning-swath`.
  - persweep_s0: per-sweep, per-channel NNLS, each bin's own demultiplexed signal.
  - persweep_s1: the same with 3 bins centered on each bin, under the 1.18 Th window label.
  - Channels come from maxima of the intensity-weighted m/z histogram (10 ppm). About 5.2M channels
    per replicate, 0.3% of ions passed through undemultiplexed, about 27 min per replicate on
    4 workers.
- **DIA-NN:** 2.3.2 with the CarafeSharp library `ztscan_carafe_lib.parquet`, `--qvalue 0.01`.
  Entrapment FDP by FDRBench's combined estimate.

| arm | target precursors per run (A1 / D1 / G1) | entrapment FDP | replicate CV, own set (n) | replicate CV, shared 793 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw, plain | 2,659 / 2,694 / 2,627 | 0.82 / 0.96 / 0.61% | 0.101 (1,961) | 0.087 |
| raw, `--scanning-swath` | 2,768 / 2,807 / 2,958 | 0.65 / 0.78 / 0.74% | 0.104 (2,099) | 0.086 |
| persweep, own bin | 1,374 / 1,323 / 1,239 | 0.58 / 0.75 / 0.00% | 0.179 (834) | 0.180 |
| persweep, 3 bins | 2,306 / 2,304 / 2,446 | 0.52 / 0.86 / 1.54% | 0.140 (1,658) | 0.114 |
| persweep, 5 bins | 2,729 / 2,635 / 2,684 | 0.80 / 0.91 / 0.82% | 0.117 (1,995) | see below |
| persweep weighted, 3 bins | 2,441 / 2,311 / 2,459 | 0.41 / 1.12 / 0.89% | 0.126 (1,708) | see below |

Replicate CVs on the 1,395 precursors all five arms share:

| raw | scanning | 3 bins | 3 bins weighted | 5 bins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.095 | 0.095 | 0.136 | 0.120 | 0.107 |

- **Poisson weighting improves replicate precision on real data, as modeled** (0.136 to 0.120 at
  3 bins), though by less than the model's factor.
- It barely changes identifications (+2%). The same low-abundance precursors are lost: 57% of the
  lowest raw-quantity quartile kept, against 56% unweighted.

- **5 bins (5.9 Th) matches raw identifications and FDP**, at about half the raw specificity width
  (the transmission is about 11 Th), with a small CV cost.
- **In the model, 5 bins trades accuracy for precision:**

  | ZT model | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | unchanged scatter | XIC r |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | none | 0.98 | 0.45 | 0.48 | 0.028 | 0.93 |
  | per-sweep, 3 bins | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.82 | 0.078 | 0.96 |
  | per-sweep, 5 bins | 0.37 | 0.22 | 0.58 | 0.039 | 0.97 |

  - Five bins let most of the ratio compression back (slope 0.58 against 0.82), but halve the
    added noise, which is why identifications recover.
  - The output width is a real choice: 3 bins for accuracy, 5 bins for identification and
    precision.
  - Poisson weighting at 3 bins recovers part of the precision, but not the identifications.

- **The real data follows the model's pattern on output width.**
  - The own-bin output halves identifications, as the earlier full-run test did (15,439 vs 27,341).
  - 3 bins centered on each bin recovers most of it: 12% below raw.
  - The model's XIC correlations predicted this: 0.74 for the own bin, 0.96 for 3 bins.
- **Replicate CVs rise with demux.** This is the precision cost the model predicts (finding 7), not
  a measurement of accuracy.
- **The remaining 3-bin loss is low-abundance, i.e. counting noise.** Of raw's targets, the 3-bin arm
  keeps:
  - 56% of the lowest quartile of raw quantity, 75%, 85%, and 95% of the highest.
  - 77-79% at every offset of the precursor from its bin center, so the 3-bin output has removed
    the placement problem.
  - 79% of 2+, 76% of 3+ and 73% of 4+.
  - It also finds about 830 precursors (over the three runs) that raw does not.
- Not measurable with this data: whether the demuxed quantities are more accurate (finding 7).

**Track 1: a narrow-window demux file** (DIA-NN and Osprey narrow the precursors each spectrum can
hold).
1. Choose the layout on the slice with DIA-NN (IDs, FDP, replicate CV, file size):

**Track 2: combine weak signal across sweeps.**
3. Positions from the pooled peak, amplitudes per sweep:
   - per channel and block, NNLS on the summed sweeps finds the active positions;
   - each sweep is then solved restricted to them, with Poisson weights;
   - there is no source count to choose.
   Model first, then the slice. Target: a smaller low-abundance loss (the 3-bin arm keeps 56% of
   the weakest quartile) and lower CVs, with model accuracy held.
4. Only if 3 leaves a clear gap: fit each source's position directly from where its signal starts
   and stops, as a continuous value shared across its peak's sweeps. This also removes the grid
   problem.

**Then:**
5. The C# port in Osprey.Demux: channels, weighted per-sweep NNLS with the cross-sweep step,
   per-file calibration, and the writer. One demuxed file shared by Osprey and Skyline; about
   100x faster per channel than the Python.
6. Ask the lab for known-ratio ZT Scan data (a two- or three-proteome mix, or ZT Scan and Zeno SWATH

## Night 2: solver choice, and the C# port (2026-09-26)

**Solver, with 500 targets (ZT Scan, background 20,000, 3-bin output):**

| | \|log2 A\| | FC err | FC slope | unchanged scatter | lowest third: unchanged / changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 1.363 | 0.621 | 0.48 | 0.039 | 0.088 / 0.908 |
| per-sweep | 0.376 | 0.248 | 0.72 | 0.086 | 0.192 / 0.605 |
| per-sweep, weighted | 0.340 | 0.210 | 0.69 | 0.064 | 0.209 / 0.647 |
| positions pooled over +/-1 sweep, weighted | 0.357 | 0.201 | 0.72 | 0.093 | 0.272 / 0.603 |
| pooled plus own positions, weighted | 0.376 | 0.219 | 0.71 | 0.078 | 0.218 / 0.622 |

- Per-sweep with Poisson weights is the most balanced, and pooling positions across sweeps does not
  improve on it. The 100-target runs' lowest-third numbers were noise.
- **Staggered, 500 targets:** the same weights help without a floor (FC err 0.125 to 0.110,
  |log2 A| 0.201 to 0.167) and are neutral with the 8-ion floor (0.157 to 0.152).
- So one strategy, per-channel NNLS with Poisson weights, serves both instruments.

**The C# port.** Branch `Skyline/work/20260926_osprey_ztscan_persweep` in `D:\Dev\pwiz-osprey-demux`,
based on PR #4710's head. Local commits only:
- c7f20cdd74: ScanningDemultiplexer, FragmentChannelFinder, ScanningLayout, Osprey.DemuxTool.
- 0f217a3331: the staggered path, and reading vendor files.
- f275c565aa: the sparse Gram build and the incremental NNLS factor.
- Gate on each: all 611 Osprey tests pass, and inspection is clean.

What each piece does:
- **ScanningDemultiplexer.DemuxUnit** (ZT Scan): channels from the block's m/z histogram; per channel
  and sweep, NNLS on the measured transmission, then a Poisson-weighted refit, applied as a low-rank
  update of A^T A.
- **DemuxInterpolatedUnit** (staggered): the same channels and solve, with every window's channel
  makima-interpolated to each spectrum's time.
- **Osprey.DemuxTool:**
  - reads .wiff2 and .raw directly, vendor-centroided like msconvert's `peakPicking vendor`;
  - `--scheme scanning --layout centered:k|tiled:k`;
  - `--scheme staggered` writes one spectrum per bin of each window, as msconvert does.

What the checks found:
- **Reading .wiff2 directly** reproduces msconvert's centroided mzML spectra exactly (cosine
  1.0000). It needed the vendor readers registered, and the Sciex.Wiff2 plugin and its SQLite
  natives staged. Osprey itself will need the same staging to read .wiff2.
- **C# against the Python prototype** on the A1 slice (tiled:5): 4,250 spectra, median cosine
  0.9997, 99.6% at 0.99 or better, total intensity ratio 1.0001. The channel counts agree
  (5,192,520 against 5,192,534).
- **Speed:** the slice took 591 s on 8 threads while other jobs shared the machine. The solve
  dominates: the weighted refit doubled it, and dense loops over A were the cost, not the NNLS
  factorization. The sparse rewrite is committed; its speed is not yet measured on a quiet machine.

- **Not writing solved values under one ion lifts identifications by 7%.** That is 8% over raw and
  above DIA-NN's scanning mode (2,768-2,958), at lower FDP.

  observed spectra of a scanning bin carry about 16 positions' worth of signal, so the share kept is
  small and noisy.

### Eclipse, full EV13 + EV14 from .raw, searched with Osprey

**Writing solved intensities failed on the Orbitrap for two reasons:**
- Spectra carried twice msconvert's peaks. Solved values were written down to 0.2 "ions", which at
  100 counts per ion is 20 counts, far under the Orbitrap floor of about 780.
- 30% of the strong peaks moved more than 3 ppm. Every peak of a 10 ppm channel was written at the
  channel's mean m/z.

**Apportioning fixes both.** Each observed peak is split among its window's bins by the solution's
shares, at its own m/z, as pwiz does.
- With that, the same channels and weighted solve beat both msconvert and PR #4710, at equal
  entrapment FDP: 52 entrapment hits against 54.
- The new path shares 31,327 peptides with msconvert, adds 3,174 of its own and misses 1,818.
- Demux took 823 s and 555 s per run on a shared machine, since only spectra where a channel was
  observed are solved.

## What this means for the design

1. **Product-ion-level demux is sound for quantitation on both instruments.** In a realistic
   background it cuts fold-change error by more than half and removes most of the ratio compression.
   No grouping of fragments is needed.
2. **ZT Scan output: framed:3:1 on the slice**, meaning 3.5 Th windows each carrying the
   demultiplexed signal of 5 positions (5.9 Th). Over a whole run, with the floor, it lost 1.2%
   against plain, so the layout is open again (morning of 2026-09-27, above).
   - Per-channel placement into one 1.18 Th bin is not recoverable under counting noise, in the model
     or on real data: identifications halve.
   - Weighted per-sweep demux gives about +5% identifications over raw on the dense slice, at raw's
     precision, with files 2.8x smaller than one spectrum per bin.
   - Tiled windows without the margin lose precursors near window edges.
   - A 1-ion output floor adds 3-7% more identifications at a precision cost, so floor the search
     file and quantify unfloored. framed:3:1 with the floor writes a 0.23 GB slice file against
     centered:5's 1.51 GB unfloored.
   - Over a whole run the gain shrinks to +1.7%: it comes where the data is dense, and early-gradient
     regions lose.
3. **Staggered output: apportion observed peaks.** Each observed peak is split among its window's
   bins by the solved shares, at its own m/z. With the new channels and weights this beats msconvert
   by 4.2% and PR #4710 by 1.7% on Eclipse, at equal FDP. Writing solved intensities failed on the
   Orbitrap.
3b. **Solver for both: per-channel NNLS with Poisson row weights** (finding 3b; 500-target check in
   Night 2). Nothing else tried beat it at realistic density.
   - The separable time model loses, partly because its rank selection absorbs weak sources. With
     AIC it still trails (finding 3).
   - The spec's §4.3 roughness penalty spreads intensity across bins and should be dropped. The
     useful time structure is "a source does not move in m/z", which is the separable model.
4. **Orbitrap staggered: interpolate-then-solve**, not the separable model.
   - The separable model is a small refinement without a charge floor and worse with one
     (finding 2b).
   - The new C# path (histogram channels, Poisson weights, apportioned output) edges out PR #4710's
     port on Eclipse (+1.7%).
   - So PR #4710 could adopt the weights and channels once the new path is reviewed.
5. **Calibrate the ZT Scan transmission per file** (spec §2.4). A 10% width error visibly costs
   accuracy, most when the real transmission is wider than assumed.
6. **Drop the shared rho prior** (finding 5).
7. **Validation needs known-ratio data.** Replicates show the demux precision cost (and on ZT Scan an
   identification cost at low abundance) but cannot show the accuracy gain. Ask the lab for a two- or
   three-proteome ZT Scan run, or ZT Scan and Zeno SWATH on the same sample.
8. **Speed:** the Python per-sweep prototype takes about 27 min per 2-minute, 200 m/z slice on
   4 workers (5.2M channels). A full run is about 20x that, so the C# implementation needs to be
   roughly two orders of magnitude faster per channel: vectorized NNLS on the small blocks, and
   reuse of the factorization per bin geometry, as the spec proposes.
